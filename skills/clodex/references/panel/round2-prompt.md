## What to produce

For each answer, in order:

1. **Quote the specific claim you are attacking.** Not a summary of their
   position — the actual sentence. A critique that cannot name its target is
   not a critique.
2. **State what is wrong with it**, and how you know: a counter-example, a
   failure case, a contradicted fact, a missing constraint. If you can execute
   something that disproves it, execute it and report the output.
3. **Rate its severity**: fatal (the conclusion does not survive) · material
   (weakens it) · minor (worth noting).

Then, separately:

4. **What they got right that you missed.** Say so plainly. A panellist who
   finds nothing to concede in two other answers is not reading them.
5. **Whether your own position survives.** If one of them broke it, say so —
   changing your mind under evidence is the correct outcome, not a loss.

## Rules

- ⚠️ **You are not trying to reach agreement.** There is no further round and no
  consensus step. Debating toward a shared answer measurably performs *worse*
  than independent answers judged on evidence — a confidently wrong argument
  drags correct ones with it. State your position and stop.
- Attack the argument, never the phrasing. Style is not a defect.
- If an answer is simply correct, say that in one line and move on. Manufacturing
  a criticism to seem rigorous is the failure mode this whole design guards
  against.
- Confidence is not evidence — in theirs or in yours.
